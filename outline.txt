TITLE: Text Me, Lord
GENRE: Christian YA Romantic Comedy
TONE: Warm, funny, heartfelt, and faith-filled, with a witty and observant teenage voice that stays honest about insecurity. Light and relatable, never preachy. Faith is shown as honest and thoughtful, not as a way to predict romantic outcomes.
STRUCTURE: Each chapter is written in 3 parts of about 800 words each, for about 2,400 words per chapter.

MAIN CHARACTERS:
- Naomi: 17. Funny, observant, and more sensitive than she lets people see. She gives excellent advice to everyone except herself. Her journey is about learning emotional honesty, building healthier friendships, and trusting God without expecting every uncertainty to end the way she wants.
- Daniel: 17. Thoughtful and quietly funny. He likes Naomi but avoids uncomfortable conversations because he hates disappointing people. His journey involves learning to communicate directly and honestly, even when the truth might disappoint someone.
- Ruth: Naomi's close friend. She loves Naomi but grows tired of being an audience for her romantic anxiety. She has her own private disappointments and ambitions and needs Naomi to recognize her as a whole person.
- Naomi's Family: Her everyday family life provides emotional grounding. School, responsibilities, family disagreements, church, and friendship remain important throughout the story.
- The Mentor: An older woman at church whom Naomi respects. She asks honest questions instead of giving easy answers and never shames Naomi.

MAIN PLOT SUMMARY:
Seventeen-year-old Naomi is an expert at giving relationship advice but terrible at following it when it comes to her own crush on Daniel. Every message, delayed reply, and punctuation mark becomes evidence she uses to guess how he feels about her. As her romantic anxiety begins affecting her friendship with Ruth, her school responsibilities, and her peace of mind, Naomi must confront her fear that being chosen by a boy is what makes her worthy of love. Through honest conversations, painful misunderstandings, faith, and personal growth, Naomi learns that prayer is not a way to control romantic outcomes, friendship requires mutual care, and love needs honesty rather than constant guessing. Daniel must also confront his habit of avoiding difficult conversations. By the end, Naomi and Daniel decide to explore a relationship, but Naomi's greatest victory is discovering that she can care about someone deeply without allowing his attention to define her identity, faith, or happiness.

Chapter 8: I Sent the Message Anyway.
